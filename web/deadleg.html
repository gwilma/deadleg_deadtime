What the model does

When the tap opens, hot water displaces the cold water in the pipe as a plug. The leading hot water gives up heat to the cold wall, so the front that reaches the tap is cooler than the supply and arrives later than the plug-flow time. The model follows that exchange in each short length of pipe: forced convection from the water to the inside of the wall, conduction through the wall in thin layers, and natural convection plus radiation from the outside of the wall to one well-mixed body of air, which warms in turn.

Copper is thin and conducts so well that it acts as a single heat store; the front is delayed by about the ratio of wall to water heat capacity, the σ above. Plastic conducts about a thousand times worse, so only a thin inner skin warms during a draw. Warm water reaches the tap close to the plug-flow time, but the last few degrees wait on heat soaking into the wall.

Water properties vary with temperature; the film coefficients use the Gnielinski correlation inside (blended to laminar flow below a Reynolds number of 10,000) and Churchill–Chu natural convection plus linearised radiation outside. The derivation, with every term defined, is in the project’s physics paper.
